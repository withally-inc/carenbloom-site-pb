From Central MTR

Use Exit D1 or D2 for the Queen’s Road Central side. Head west along Queen’s Road Central, then uphill on D’Aguilar Street to Wyndham Street and number 29. Allow about 7 to 10 minutes from the exit.

Central station map ↗

From the airport, by train

Take the Airport Express to Hong Kong Station, about 24 minutes on the train. With bags, take a taxi from the station. Travelling light? Follow the pedestrian connection towards Central Station and the D exits, then the route above. Allow about 15 to 20 minutes on foot from Hong Kong Station.

Airport Express ↗

From the airport, by taxi

Follow signs to the official taxi rank and choose a red urban taxi for Hong Kong Island. Show the driver “LKF 29, 29 Wyndham Street, Central”. Allow roughly 40 to 60 minutes, with extra time in traffic.

Taxi areas ↗

Travel times are planning estimates, not live directions. The last stretch is uphill; a taxi is easier with luggage.

A little local knowledge

Arrive with the basics.

Save the address. Charge your phone. The rest gets easier from here.

Octopus & contactless

Octopus is handy for public transport and small purchases. On the MTR, use a marked contactless gate with a supported bank card, and tap in and out with the same card or device.

MTR payment guide ↗

The MTR is your friend

Central serves the Island and Tsuen Wan lines. Sheung Wan and Admiralty are one stop away in opposite directions. Sai Ying Pun is two stops west. Follow the exit letters, not just the station name.

MTR network map ↗

Taxis & ride-hailing

Use a red urban taxi for Central, or choose the Taxi option in Uber. Show the driver the building address. Check the payment method before setting off and keep some Hong Kong dollars handy.

Taxi options ↗

Pack a layer

November is usually mild; December is cooler. Average daily lows and highs are about 20 to 25°C in November and 16 to 20°C in December. Bring a light jacket and check the forecast before you fly.

Seasonal weather ↗

Plugs & power

Bring a Type G adaptor, the three rectangular pins used in the UK. Hong Kong runs on 220V, 50Hz. Check that your devices support the voltage; an adaptor does not convert it.

Plug guide ↗

Stay connected

Set up a Hong Kong SIM or eSIM for maps on the move. Check device compatibility and the provider’s activation steps. Local prepaid SIMs require registration. Airport and public Wi-Fi can help you get started.

Connectivity guide ↗

The bill, without the mystery

Many restaurants add a 10% service charge. Check the bill before adding a tip. Extra tipping is your choice; at casual local cafés it is not expected.

Tipping guide ↗

A short walk can be uphill

Central looks compact on a map. Slopes, steps and crossings add time. Wear shoes you can walk in, and take a taxi for the last stretch if luggage or mobility makes the hills inconvenient.
