# Care & Bloom brand guideline

Version 4.0, 21 October 2026. Owner: Care & Bloom Limited, Hong Kong. Maintained by Melriver.
This document turns the approved v4 direction (`docs/BRIEF.md`) into a guideline that holds outside the website: decks, recruiting, email, signage, sub-brands. Where this file and BRIEF.md disagree on a web build detail, BRIEF.md wins and this file gets corrected. The visual edition is `brandbook/brandbook.html`.

## 1. The idea

Care & Bloom builds consumer health brands in the categories where trust is hardest to earn: sexual health first, then longevity, mental health and education. The name carries the whole brand. **Care** is the rigor: the record stated plainly (1,000,000+ customers, 9 figures, under 36 months, profitably), clean type, calm surfaces, and a respect for the stakes of health. **Bloom** is the growth and the wit: the rose opening in a hand, the one italic word in a headline, the lemon that follows your cursor, a spaceship in the careers copy. The system keeps the two in a fixed ratio. Almost everything is calm, sharp and factual, so that the one wink on a page lands. A visitor should leave thinking two things: these people know their numbers, and these people are fun to work with.

Heritage: Hello Nancy's 2022 style guide was loud by design (Tickled Pink, Electric Lime, a script wordmark). It already had a "business attire" register of neutrals for decks and formal reports. Care & Bloom is that register grown into a group: the parent dresses quietly so its brands can be loud.

## 2. Positioning lines

Each line is assembled from copy already on carenbloom.com. No new claims.

1. **"We build enormous brands where trust is hardest to earn."** Hero line plus brands intro. Recommended default: ambition and category in one sentence.
2. **"Products people can believe in, from brands that respect the stakes."** From the spaces intro. For partners, press and regulated contexts.
3. **"1,000,000+ customers in under 36 months, profitably."** The record as positioning. For investor and recruiting surfaces where proof leads.

The manifesto "We move fast. We experiment. We build enormous brands." stays the hero and is not a tagline: it is never placed under the logo.

## 3. Voice and tone

The voice is an operator who is good at their job and is not trying to impress you. Tone shifts by audience; the voice does not.

| Audience | Tone | Ratio of wit |
|---|---|---|
| Candidates | Direct, warm, demanding | One wink per section |
| Partners, investors | Precise, record first | One wink per page, or none |
| Press | Plain, quotable | None in facts, one in the headline |
| Customers (via sub-brands) | Their brand's voice | Their brand's rules |

### Principles

**State the record, skip the adjective.**
Do: "1,000,000+ customers in under 36 months, profitably."
Don't: "Explosive, category-defining growth across Asia."

**Name the category plainly.** Stigma survives on euphemism. We say sexual health, pleasure, fertility, hormones.
Do: "Care for intimacy, hormones, fertility, pleasure, and agency still asks people to navigate stigma."
Don't: "Adult wellness for your special moments."

**Respect the stakes.** Health copy never promises an outcome it cannot prove. No cure, fix, hack or guarantee language.
Do: "Longevity is moving from abstract promises to practical healthspan: energy, recovery, prevention."
Don't: "Add ten years to your life." / "Hack your hormones."

**Talk to operators like operators.** Recruiting copy describes the work, the number and the standard.
Do: "Below floor, fix it yourself today."
Don't: "We're a family of rockstars who work hard and play hard."

**Show the object, skip the shame.** Products are described as objects with a design intent.
Do: "Objects made to be picked up, understood and bought without apology."
Don't: "Discreet packaging so nobody ever knows."

**One wink, earned.** The joke sits on top of a fact, never in place of one.
Do: "Join the spaceship." above the live count of open roles.
Don't: "Ready for liftoff? Strap in, astronaut! 🚀"

### Humor rules

Wit is allowed about: ourselves and our ambition (enormous, spaceship); the way we work (the lemon, cycle time, (re)org); objects and design (Biird's "without apology"); the awkwardness of the category as a social fact, never as a person's experience.

Wit never goes near: a customer's body, sexual performance or function, fertility, pregnancy or loss, hormones and menopause symptoms, mental health conditions, aging and fear of decline, weight, illness, a candidate's rejection or a person's job loss, and anything that reads as a medical claim. No innuendo, ever, including in recruiting posts. No emoji in group copy.

The test: would the line still land if the reader were the person it is about, on their worst day? If not, cut it.

## 4. Writing mechanics

- **No em dashes or en dashes.** Use a colon to introduce, a comma to continue, a full stop to end. Ranges use "to": "15 to 20 roles", "Monday to Friday". A hyphen only joins words: "player-coach", "co-pilot".
- **A sentence stops when the information is given.** No trailing comment clause after a comma, no "and that matters", no announced counts ("three things"), no setups like "here is the thing".
- **Spelling: American.** The current site already leans American (behavior, prioritized, judgment); one word is British (stigmatised). The audience is international operators and investors, and US English is the default of the tech and DTC talent pool we recruit from. Normalize to: behavior, color, center, organize, stigmatized, judgment. Keep proper names as their owners spell them.
- **Oxford comma**, as the current copy does: "longevity, mental health, and education".
- **Case.** Sentence case everywhere: headlines, buttons, nav, labels in source. Labels display uppercase through CSS only.
- **Names.** "Care & Bloom" always with the ampersand. Never "Care and Bloom", "C&B", "CnB", "Carenbloom" (the domain carenbloom.com appears only as a URL). Legal: "Care & Bloom Limited". Brands: "Hello Nancy", "Biird". People: full name on first mention.
- **Numbers.** Numerals for every metric and every quantity of 10 or more; words for one to nine in running prose ("two consumer brands"), numerals in stats ("2 consumer brands"). Thousands with commas: 1,000,000. A floor takes a plus: 1,000,000+. Money: "$100M", "$100M ARR", capital M, no space, USD unless marked ("HK$"). Percent: "34%". Durations in prose: "every 23 seconds"; in the live counter the existing "+1 ≈ every 23 s" stays as written.
- **Dates.** Prose: "November 2, 2026". Labels and role rows: "Closes Nov 2". Never 02/11.
- **Numbering** appears only where order is real: values 01 to 07. Sections are named with words.
- **Arrows** are type, from PP Mori: → for internal links, ↗ for external ones. Never both on one link.

## 5. Logo

### Construction (wordmark C)

The lockup is the four-petal mark plus the word "Care & Bloom" in PP Mori SemiBold 600, sentence case, tracking -0.02em, with the ampersand in Newsreader italic 400 at 1.08em, nudged down 0.02em so its baseline sits optically on the Mori baseline.

Let **P**, the petal unit, be half the mark's height: the distance from the mark's center to the tip of one petal.

- Mark height = 2P = 1.45 × the cap height of the wordmark.
- Gap from the mark's right edge to the "C" = 0.9P (0.45 × mark height).
- The mark is vertically centered on the middle of the cap height, so the lower petal drops slightly below the baseline. This is intended.
- These ratios match the outlined masters in `public/brand/` generated by `scripts/brand/brand.mjs`. Never rebuild the lockup by eye.

On the web the lockup is live text plus the inline SVG mark. Outlined SVG files are for OG images, favicons, print and partners.

### Clear space

1P on all four sides of the lockup, measured from the outermost ink (petal tips, the "m"). 2P when the logo sits alone on a cover, sign or slide. Nothing enters that zone, including image edges and other logos.

### Minimum sizes

| Element | Screen | Print |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal lockup | 18px font size (mark 18px tall) | 28mm wide |
| Mark alone | 16px | 6mm |
| Endorsement line | 11px | 2mm cap height |

Below 16px the petal counters fill in. Use the mark alone at 16px favicon size, and never the lockup below 18px.

### The mark alone

The mark is the favicon, app icon, preloader, nav button and social avatar. It is used alone when the name is already present (the nav while reading, footer sign-off) or the surface is too small for the lockup. It is never redrawn, outlined, filled in solid, or used as a letter. The only motion allowed on it is the bloom (petals open from the center), the 45° turn on hover, and, in the nav only, the compass: the petal of the section being read stays full while the other three fade to 26% (opacity only, never reshaped).

### Color versions

All versions are single color. The ampersand is never a second color.

| Version | Use |
|---|---|
| Ink #0A1220 on paper, white or mist | Primary. Default everywhere |
| Mist #DCEDF5 on ink | The one dark section, dark slides, signage on dark walls |
| Cobalt #2331E4 on paper or white | Mark alone in a live or hover state; the full lockup only in single-color recruiting graphics |
| Ink on sun | Never. Sun is a highlighter, not a ground for the logo |

Over photography, the logo goes in ink or mist on a calm area with at least 7:1 local contrast, else not at all.

### Misuse

1. Two-tone lockup (cobalt ampersand: that was variant D, rejected).
2. Uppercase "CARE & BLOOM" in the logo.
3. Ampersand in PP Mori, or the whole word in Newsreader.
4. Stretching, condensing, rotating, slanting.
5. Rearranging: mark on the right, stacked words, mark between words.
6. Outlines, shadows, gradients, glass, halftone applied to the logo.
7. Logo on sun, on cobalt, on busy photography.
8. Retyping the lockup at a different tracking or weight.
9. Mark recolored in a sub-brand palette.
10. "Care & Bloom" written next to the mark in any typeface other than PP Mori.

**The night moment.** People keeps `.surface-ink`, whose ground is now `--night`: in light mode it is ink as before; in violet mode it drops to near-black violet #050310, below the violet ground, so scrolling into People still reads as the lights going down. The nav takes the same night ground over it.

**The highlighter.** On a dark ground the half-height band would leave the top of the letters light on violet and the bottom light on sun (1.12:1). In violet mode `.hl` (and every sun underline, through `--hl-y`, `--hl-h`, `--hl-fg`) grows to cover the word and its type turns `--on-sun` as the band arrives.

**The logo** is lavender mist (`--ink`) on violet, single colour, as mist on ink is in light. It never goes cobalt on violet except in the hover and focus state of the mark.

**Imagery.** Halftone art is re-inked, not inverted: ink dots become lavender-mist dots (#D9D2F5), cobalt becomes a brighter violet-blue (#5F5BFF), sun stays sun, paper becomes the violet ground. Portraits: the ink print maps night to lavender, the cobalt print violet-blue to lavender. Proof logos turn lavender mist. Products stay full colour. Files and the generator: `docs/ASSETS.md`, "Violet mode".

**The switch.** On desktop a click on the mark in the header switches the register (the wordmark is the home link). The mark turns a quarter while the new theme floods from its centre in a four-petal shape that opens and turns 45° as it grows, 720ms on the house curve, until the cusps between the petals pass the farthest corner. It is a View Transition of the root only (no named elements); without View Transitions the new ground floods and the page flips under it. Reduced motion: a 200ms crossfade. Below 960px the mark opens the menu, so the menu carries a labelled "Violet mode" switch whose knob is the mark (it slides and turns a quarter). The mark's accessible name says what it will do ("Switch to violet mode" / "Switch to light mode") with `aria-pressed`. It is a fifth gesture of the mark, after the bloom, the hover turn and the compass, and the only one that changes the page.

**Default.** A stored choice wins; with none, `prefers-color-scheme: dark` means violet. The choice persists in `localStorage` (`cb-theme`) and is applied before first paint.

## 7. Typography

### Families

- **PP Mori SemiBold 600**: display, headings, labels, the wordmark. The voice of the record.
- **PP Mori Regular 400**: body, UI, captions.
- **Newsreader Italic** 400 for the wink word and the logo ampersand, 300 for quotes. The voice of the wit.
- No monospace anywhere. Azeret Mono is retired.
- Fallbacks: PP Mori → "Helvetica Neue", Arial (metric-matched with size-adjust on the web); Newsreader → Georgia. In email and Office documents where the brand fonts cannot travel: Arial for everything, Georgia italic for the wink.

### Scale

| Role | Size | Leading | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display | clamp(64px, 13vw, 208px) | 0.95 | -0.03em |
| H1 | clamp(44px, 7vw, 104px) | 1.0 | -0.03em |
| H2 | clamp(34px, 4.6vw, 64px) | 1.02 | -0.02em |
| H3 | clamp(22px, 2vw, 28px) | 1.1 | -0.015em |
| Lede | 21 to 24px | 1.45 | 0 |
| Body | 18px | 1.6 | 0 |
| Small | 15px | 1.5 | 0 |
| Caption | 14px | 1.5 | 0 |
| Label | 12px, uppercase | 1.2 | 0.12em |
| Quote | Newsreader 300 italic, 24 to 34px | 1.2 | -0.01em |

Body measure: 62 characters maximum. Headlines use `text-wrap: balance`; body uses `text-wrap: pretty`.

### The wink-word rule

One word per headline may be set in Newsreader italic 400 at 1.08em, same color as the headline. Never two. Many headlines have none.

The wink goes on the word that carries the feeling or the joke: usually the noun or adjective at the end of the line.

Good:
- We move fast. We experiment. We build *enormous* brands.
- Join the *spaceship*.
- Start a *conversation*.
- Focused teams. Player *coaches*.

Bad:
- We *build* *enormous* brands. (two winks)
- *1,000,000* customers. (never on a number)
- Meet *Care & Bloom*. (never on a brand name)
- Visit *brand* ↗ (never in buttons, links, nav, labels or body)
- Your *fertility*, handled. (never on a health word: see humor rules)
- We build ENORMOUS brands in italic caps. (the wink is lowercase or sentence case only)

Never add a highlight, color or underline on the wink word: it already carries the emphasis. One exception, approved on 21 October 2026: the record headline "In under 36 months, *profitably*.", where the sun underline draws on *profitably* as the payoff of the 36-month strip. It stays the only one on the site.

### Numbers

Records and counters: PP Mori 600, `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`, tracking -0.04em, leading 1. The plus of a floor sits at the same size. Units after a number drop to body or label size ("36 months"). Numbers never take the wink and never go in Newsreader.

## 8. Layout and grid

- 12 columns, max content width 1280px, gutter 24px (16px under 768px). Side padding clamp(16px, 4vw, 48px). At 390px wide, 16px gutters and no horizontal scroll.
- Spacing scale (px): 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 72, 100, 144. Section rhythm 144px desktop, 96px mobile.
- Reading column: 62ch for body, 72ch for long documents.
- Radii: cards 18px, images 12 to 18px, chips and buttons 999px, inputs 12px.
- The grid is never drawn on screen. v3's visible vertical lines are retired.
- Asymmetry over centering: headlines left-aligned, a sticky label column on the left is allowed for long sections.
- One dark section per page. On slides, one dark slide per chapter break.

## 9. Iconography: the petal system

The petal is the group's one ownable shape. A single petal is one quarter of the mark: the shape from the center ring to one tip. It is used as a system, sparingly.

| Use | Spec |
|---|---|
| Bullet | One petal, 0.5em, rotated 45°, ink; cobalt only when the item is live |
| Loader | The four petals open in sequence from the center, 1.2s loop, expo.out. Replaces every spinner |
| Divider | The mark at 24px, centered, with 72px space above and below. Long documents and decks only, never on the website |
| Pattern | Mark lattice on a 4P grid, alternating 0° and 45°, ink at 4% on paper or mist at 6% on ink. Print, packaging tissue, office glass. Never behind text on screen |
| Status | Live dot: 8px circle, cobalt on paper, sun ground with ink dot inside a chip |

No pictogram set on the website. Arrows are type. If a product UI needs icons, use a 1.5px stroke set at 20px with round joins in ink, and never draw a petal into it.

## 10. Imagery

### House treatment: halftone duotone

The house photographic treatment is a halftone screen mapped to two inks: shadows Ink #0A1220 to Cobalt #2331E4, highlights to Paper or Mist. Screen angle 45°, dots visible at 1:1 viewing distance (roughly a 6px cell at 1440px wide). Sun may appear as a highlight inside a halftone, under 5% of the image, as in the blooming rose.

| Subject | Treatment |
|---|---|
| People (leaders, team, candidates' future colleagues) | Cobalt duotone |
| The hero motif and editorial or abstract images | Halftone, ink hand with cobalt subject, optional sun highlight |
| Products | Full color, the sub-brand's own photography. Never duotone a product: it misstates the object |
| Sub-brand campaigns | Their own palette, untouched on group surfaces |
| Lemon | Halftone grayscale, one per page, as a character |

### The hero motif

One hand holding one flower: the rose closed, then blooming. It is the visual form of the name and appears once per surface (site hero, deck cover, recruiting header). The hand is grayscale halftone, the flower cobalt. Future verticals keep the hand and the composition; the object in the hand may change (a sprig for longevity, an open book for education), the rose stays the group's own.

### Photography direction for new shoots

- **Casting.** The real team where possible. Ages 25 to 55, the four cities' actual mix (Hong Kong, Shenzhen, London, San Francisco). Hands matter as much as faces: unmanicured, real, varied.
- **Light.** Soft daylight from one side, high-key grounds near paper or mist so the duotone holds. No hard flash, no colored gels.
- **Crop.** Portraits head and shoulders on a clean ground, eyes on the upper third, 30% free space above for type. Hands and objects cropped tight, the object on the vertical center line.
- **Moments.** People working: a whiteboard, a scorecard, two people over one laptop. Never posed handshakes.
- **Avoid.** Stethoscopes and white coats; pills spilling from bottles; couples in bed; fruit or emoji used as sexual metaphor on group surfaces (that is Hello Nancy's language, not the parent's); beige wellness flat lays; blurred bodies; AI-generated faces or hands.

## 11. Motion

Motion carries the wit, so it follows the same discipline as the copy.

1. **One curve.** expo.out, CSS cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1), entrances about 1.1s. One spring for what people touch.
2. **Buttons stay still.** Color change only on hover, press scale .97.
3. **Links get the dot.** A cobalt dot arrives on the left and pushes the label; the underline draws from the left. Nothing that is not clickable reacts to hover.
4. **Things rise into focus.** 16px rise, blur 6px to 0, opacity 0 to 1, stagger 60 to 80ms. Images wipe up from the bottom with an inner scale 1.08 to 1.
5. **Headlines rise line by line** from their own masks, 1.1s, 75 to 90ms stagger, 2.5° from bottom left.
6. **Numbers roll like an odometer**: left digits land first, each digit rolls only its last 3 to 5 steps. Never a long count up from zero.
7. **Nothing forced.** No scroll-jacking, no pinned horizontal sections. A short natural sticky is allowed.
8. **Nothing clipped.** Pad masks for descenders and italic overhangs.
9. **Content is visible at rest.** If scripts fail, everything shows.
10. **Reduced motion** keeps opacity only; ambient loops stop.

### The four signature moments

Only these get the spotlight. Anything else animates quietly or not at all.

- **The bloom.** The mark opens petal by petal as the preloader (first visit per session, under 1.6s, skippable), turns 45° and goes cobalt on hover, and opens again to close the page.
- **The breathing ampersand.** The italic ampersand in the hero breathes over 5s (scale 1 to 1.06, ±2°). The only permanent loop above the fold.
- **The live counter.** "1,000,000+" stays fixed; a cobalt dot pulses beside it and a small "+1" rises every 23 seconds. It never shows a total above the claim.
- **The lemon.** One halftone lemon follows the cursor with inertia in "How we work" and turns through its frames as it moves. One lemon, one page.

The nav is chrome, not a moment: a quiet line that recedes to the mark and the live count while reading and returns on scroll up. Its one wink is on phones, where the mark is the menu button and its petals unfold into the four quarters of the menu.

## 12. Sub-brands

Care & Bloom is an endorsing parent. Its brands lead on their own surfaces; the group leads on group surfaces.

- **Endorsement line:** "A Care & Bloom brand", set in PP Mori Regular with the Newsreader italic ampersand, sentence case, preceded by the mark at 1.2 × cap height. Color: the sub-brand's own ink or white, never cobalt. It is supplied as an outlined file; never retyped in the sub-brand's font.
- **Size:** endorsement cap height between 25% and 35% of the sub-brand logo's cap height, never larger than 35%. Clear space 1P of the endorsement mark.
- **Placement on brand surfaces:** footer of the website, about page, back of pack, investor and press materials, careers pages. Never on the front of pack, ads, or product pages.
- **On group surfaces** (carenbloom.com, group decks, recruiting): sub-brand logos appear in monochrome ink at 60%, matched by optical height; their color lives in their own photography inside brand cards. Group UI never uses Hello Nancy pink #FF30CC or lime #CCFD28.
- **Color separation:** a sub-brand never uses cobalt #2331E4 or sun #FDFF6D as its primary color, so the group's codes for "live" and "evidence" stay unambiguous. Hello Nancy's Electric Lime sits close to sun; the two never share a layout.
- **Never:** a combined hero lockup ("Hello Nancy by Care & Bloom"), the petal mark inside a sub-brand logo, a sub-brand logo in cobalt duotone, Care & Bloom fonts imposed on a sub-brand.
- **New verticals** (longevity, mental health, education) get their own name and palette when they launch as brands, and follow the same endorsement rules.
